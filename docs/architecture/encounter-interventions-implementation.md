# Synthetic encounter interventions implementation

This implements the enabled I0–I6 engineering scope in the [intervention plan](encounter-interventions-implementation-plan.md). It is an opt-in **local synthetic** workflow, not clinical approval or permission for real-patient issuance. Procedures and external transmission remain unavailable.

## Delivered behavior

One panel outside the template form provides a searchable type picker, typed prescription/laboratory/imaging/referral/follow-up composers, source review, filters, and paginated summaries. Saving a prescription, laboratory or imaging request, or referral adds an editable **Pending signature** draft to the encounter. The doctor reviews these requests with the exact note candidate. One **Review & sign** confirmation issues their printable documents in the same tenant transaction as the note signature; the doctor does not perform another review/authorization action for each document. Follow-up tasks retain their own ownership workflow. The Dashboard shell, shared sheet, buttons and patient context remain in use. Note and intervention edits share the encounter navigation guard. Forms retain their contents on errors; uncertain commands retain the original request body, UUIDs and idempotency key. Unmounted patient workspaces stop subsequent steps and ignore late responses.

Each unissued draft row offers **Remove** with a short inline confirmation naming the patient and draft. The clinician does not fill out a removal form. The existing versioned abandon command records a standard removal reason and audit event. Removed drafts leave the active paginated list while remaining in source and summary history; they are omitted from new note-review manifests. The intervention-set version invalidates any note candidate prepared before removal. Issued interventions use their separate cancellation and disposition workflow.

Prescribing, orders/results and care coordination own separate relational sources and immutable revisions. Their source modules validate and issue their own drafts when the encounter is signed; the note service depends only on a source-owned signing interface. The signing transaction rechecks current grants, tenant/site/patient/origin encounter, author, source version and lifecycle, required fields, medication safety and duplicate intent. A failure rolls back the note signature and every document issuance. Preparation alone does not issue a document; printing does not imply delivery, dispensing, performance, attendance or result review. Originals survive cancellation and replacement. Deliberately copied replacement drafts require a current active encounter and renewed review in the note candidate. The older individual authorization endpoints remain for historical compatibility; they are no longer part of the encounter UI path.

Versioned local ingredient/service catalogs support engineering cases. Laboratory test names and imaging request names may be entered as free text or selected from the database-backed service catalog; selected suggestions retain their code and version, while unmatched names remain uncoded. Tenant V16 retires the visible synthetic imaging suggestion and adds local demo codes for real exam names such as pelvic ultrasound, chest radiography, CT and MRI. The exam labels follow examples in [ACR procedure listings](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/3102395/Narrative/) and [RadiologyInfo chest radiography](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/info/chestrad); they are suggestions, not appropriateness recommendations. These local codes are not standardized terminology or billing identifiers. Previously saved synthetic requests remain readable against their original catalog identity. Imaging issuance still requires the clinical question, destination, priority and requested date, all checked before the note candidate is offered. The prescription form groups strength, formulation, dose/unit, route, timing, duration and quantity, shows current allergy/medication status, and attaches the latest eligible chart reconciliation when available. The legacy flat safety questionnaire was removed from the form because its write endpoint is disabled. Issuance still requires a current canonical reconciliation; the form states that interaction support is unavailable. Unknown, stale, duplicate-ingredient, known-allergy and duplicate-current-medication cases fail closed. These rules do not constitute a clinical decision-support engine.

Laboratory/imaging groups retain stable individual request identities. The encounter signature issues each completed request together, while later cancellation, fulfillment and result review remain independent. Composite requisitions require every member to have been issued and freeze the exact member versions. Original composite bytes remain available after individual lifecycle changes. Incoming reports are immutable, initially owned by their receiving clinician, and require an explicit patient/request match. Corrections link the original report and create another review obligation. Referral release, delivery, acceptance/refusal/attendance and response disposition remain separate.

Existing follow-up intent UUIDs continue through the original `encounter_followup_intent → clinical_owned_task` pipeline. Source obligations persist before optional delivery. Transfers retain the existing owner until an independently authorized clinician accepts. Accepted source/task work grants only the required patient identity and source access, not the original encounter/chart. Reconciliation preserves accepted transfers and resolved tasks. Closing care does not close these obligations. Clinical offboarding remains fenced by the existing team-access boundary; arbitrary administrative revocation is not a substitute for accepted clinical handoff.

New and edited laboratory and imaging drafts require the clinical question or reason and requested date before saving; imaging additionally requires a destination, while laboratory location is optional. Priority defaults to routine and remains checked. The server rechecks the required fields before the note candidate is offered and at issuance. Older incomplete drafts remain readable and can be corrected before signing.

## Integration and document integrity

`EncounterInterventionLinks` is the server-only context, association and manifest API. All source mutations acquire the encounter boundary before source work and append the source revision, summary, set version, audit and command receipt in one tenant transaction. No encounter-module dependency on prescribing/orders/referral implementations was introduced.

`interventionSetVersion` is independent of the note response revision and each source version. Signing captures an immutable pre-sign manifest with exact source/summary revisions. Its candidate labels pending documents as requests included in this signature; successful confirmation issues those documents and appends their issued source revisions. A material intervention change rejects stale note confirmation. Source-read restrictions also protect summary counts, manifests and artifacts; a signer cannot silently omit restricted content.

After signing, the encounter view presents the verified archived note and keeps intervention content controls closed. New signatures leave no printable-document draft behind: each eligible draft is issued or the whole signature fails. Historical unissued drafts linked to older signed encounters remain unissued and are never retroactively converted. The server rejects preparation, authorization and abandonment of a draft linked to a signed encounter, including candidates prepared before signing; creation and draft updates already require an unsigned active encounter. Issued interventions can still receive separately versioned delivery, result, follow-up and cancellation dispositions. Longitudinal allergy, reported-medication and problem corrections remain separate chart actions with their own history and cannot rewrite the signed review snapshot. A reasoned linked amendment retains the original signed bytes.

HTTP note preparation captures content first, stages/verifies local bytes outside that transaction, then finalizes against captured versions and hashes. At signing, the source modules stage and archive their printable bytes inside the tenant transaction before the note signature commits. A failed transaction can leave unattached private files; the bounded cleanup removes these later. Artifacts use private local files, immutable publication, SHA-256 verification and persisted metadata. Reprints use original bytes. Attached preparations and archives are never removed by cleanup. The worker removes at most 100 unattached files older than seven days per pass, examining at most 1,000 entries; retries can recreate detached bytes. Cleanup serializes against artifact metadata attachment. Storage failure cannot report archival success.

Historical signatures without a manifest keep their original bytes and display “No intervention snapshot captured by this version.” No historical note is rerendered or assigned a fabricated empty snapshot. Missing follow-up associations are backfilled idempotently using the original intent UUID and original receipt time.

The signed-encounter list has a patient-scoped print picker. It offers the verified note and any amendments, currently issued prescriptions and imaging requests, released referrals, and one Lab Order Form per authorized laboratory batch, including a batch with one test; the server rechecks the complete group before archiving the form. New laboratory orders always create a batch, and the UI no longer offers per-test lab printing. Earlier ungrouped laboratory requests retain their original archived bytes for audit but are not offered as patient handouts. Group document creation keeps its document ID and idempotency key through an uncertain retry. Cancelled, superseded and draft sources are not offered as patient handouts in this picker; individual non-lab source state, site, patient, encounter and artifact identity are rechecked when selected. Opening an existing source retrieves its original verified archive; the browser does not reconstruct it from current fields. New synthetic preparations use an A4 handoff layout with named Practice/site, two patient identifiers, labeled services and instructions, requester identity, and a document reference. The Lab Order Form places shared receiving facility, date, indication, priority and batch reference once above the tests; if a value differs between tests, it lists the test-specific values together under that heading. Other handouts retain their explicit demo restriction. The referral handout excludes the internal disclosure-review reason. Previously archived bytes remain unchanged. Printing remains separate from delivery and clinical follow-up, and these layouts have not been approved for real-patient use.

## Enablement, migrations and rollback

Migration inventory was checked before each additive allocation. The intervention implementation adds control-plane **V16** and tenant **V8–V11**, after the existing V15/V7 work. The later [longitudinal clinical-list extension](clinical-lists-reconciliation-implementation-plan.md) adds control-plane V18 and tenant V12–V14, the prescribing catalog/refill extension adds tenant V15, imaging request suggestions add tenant V16, encounter vital sets add tenant V17, mistaken-entry removal adds tenant V18, and draft soft deletion adds tenant V19, so runtime clinical routing now requires **tenant schema 19**. New Practice provisioning applies the migrations without granting clinical access. The synthetic local seeder upgrades only its configured demo Practice.

Tenant V15 adds optional strength, form, dose unit and quantity unit to the synthetic prescribing catalog, plus immutable per-item links to an earlier issued prescription item. The prescription composer accepts a typed medicine name with catalog suggestions; an exact catalog match fills those product fields, while unmatched names retain clinician-entered values. Each of up to 30 items is explicitly new or a refill. Selecting a refill copies one issued item into a new draft and stores its source IDs; the old issued record remains unchanged. The server rejects a refill with a changed medicine identity, a missing or non-issued source, or duplicate source links. New and refill items share one versioned, idempotent prescription draft and are issued by the encounter signature. The existing clinical-list reconciliation, allergy/current-medication checks, deliberate duplicate-intent reason and synthetic issuance gate still apply. Recent refill choices in the UI come from the first page of author/site-scoped prescription history; older or other-author records are not yet selectable there.

From the repository root, with the existing local PostgreSQL environment running:

```sh
./api/mvnw -f api/pom.xml spring-boot:run \
  -Dspring-boot.run.profiles=local \
  -Dspring-boot.run.arguments='--epr.clinical.enabled=true --epr.clinical.demo-seed=true --epr.interventions.enabled=true --epr.team.enabled=true --server.port=8087'
EPR_API_PROXY_TARGET=http://127.0.0.1:8087 npm --prefix web run dev -- --port 5177
```

Use the existing documented synthetic clinician account. Never use this environment for real patient data. `EPR_INTERVENTIONS_ENABLED` controls new preparation/issuance; the per-Practice `practice_intervention_capability` flags independently control each kind. Explicit actor/site `practice_intervention_grant` rows are still required. Neither provider support nor Practice administration grants clinical authority. Seed retries do not reinstate removed permissions.

Turning off new interventions retains authorized read/history and `CONTINUE_EXISTING` dispositions, original documents and durable obligations. Turning off a kind’s preparation/authorization capability similarly stops new decisions while preserving existing clinical work. Membership, account, site and source-read revocation still deny reads and replay. Do not disable the broader clinical application or revoke the sole clinical owner as a rollout substitute.

Upgrade other synthetic Practices through the existing versioned support schema-upgrade operation, one observed canary first. Verify tenant identity and Flyway checksums, then proceed serially. Failed/mismatched routes remain quarantined; recover using the existing schema rollout workflow. Do not edit an applied migration or point a runtime role at another Practice database. See [encounter rollout](encounter-templates-obgyn-implementation.md#additive-schema-rollout-and-rollback).

## Delivery and recovery

The local worker runs every 30 seconds by default. `EPR_INTERVENTIONS_WORKER_ENABLED=false` stops automatic delivery while preserving the sources and the explicit reconciliation command. Authorized tenant scopes are resolved afresh; a PostgreSQL advisory claim prevents duplicate delivery in a scope. `clinical_delivery_checkpoint` records attempts, next attempt, last success and a non-clinical error code. Failure backoff starts at 30 seconds and is capped at one hour. Monitor failed/overdue checkpoints through an authorized tenant operational connection; no patient content belongs in monitoring labels.

Investigate route/grant/storage failures, restore the dependency and allow retry. Explicit reconciliation is idempotent. Do not delete source obligations or reconstruct them from narrative text. A revoked/unavailable scope is not bypassed by the worker; clinical access changes require the existing offboarding/handoff controls. Production fallback coverage, alert escalation and fleet worker operations require deployment validation.

## Deployment gates retained

Real-patient issuance; approved medication/service terminology and outputs; production prescriber/requester authority and signer evidence; clinical decision support; private production storage/scanning and external delivery integrations; MFA and production session controls; complete clinician offboarding/fallback coverage; production restore/downtime drills and representative clinical load; program-specific governance; and procedure capability approval remain gated. Production automated tenant-fleet migration and worker monitoring are not supplied by the local canary/SQL workflow. No offline clinical cache, offline issuance or cross-Practice chart sharing was added.
